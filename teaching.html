Teaching & tutoring

I’ve been tutoring for five years, working one-on-one with students from elementary school through university. I tutor every area of math, from elementary arithmetic, pre-algebra and algebra through geometry, trigonometry, precalculus and Calculus I, II and III. Sessions are built around the student’s own course, so we work on the material they’re seeing in class and the skills they need for exams.

With a degree in statistics, I also tutor statistics at every level: introductory statistics, probability, hypothesis testing and regression, up to advanced topics such as statistical inference and stochastic processes. I’m comfortable helping with the computing side too, including assignments in R and Python. Outside of math, I tutor philosophy and English, helping students read closely, build clear arguments and write strong essays.

Course development

At The George Washington University, I co-developed PHIL 3150: Philosophical Problems with Data, a course that brings my two fields together. It asks students to think critically about the data they use: what counts as evidence, how numbers and models can mislead, how statistical results should and shouldn’t be interpreted, and the ethical questions that come with collecting and analyzing data about people.

I also served as President of the GWU Philosophy Club, organizing discussions that brought students together around questions in philosophy and its connections to other fields.
